INTENT: A real Tokyo ramen master — hyper-realistic, not animated — works the counter of a packed Shinjuku shop at 11pm. The sequence is a controlled explosion — noodles plunged and shaken, broth launched in a steaming arc, bowl set with a thud. Multiple camera angles crash-cut fast: macro, GoPro POV, overhead, behind-the-chef tracking, side-locked. The money shot: milky broth pouring from height into the bowl in slow-motion, steam erupting in a column.

BACKGROUND: A Shinjuku counter ramen shop — eight seats, no more. Red lantern glow bleeding through the window. Dark lacquered counter wet from service. Behind the chef: stacked bowls, a bone broth vat bubbling below counter level, condensation clinging to everything. Walls plastered with handwritten kanji specials. The sound of slurping bleeds through the frame.

CAMERA: 8 crash-cut setups — GoPro over-shoulder POV behind the chef, overhead drone-locked on bowl assembly, macro rack-focus on noodles in the bamboo basket, side-tracking along the counter, low-angle looking up at the ladle mid-arc, front-facing close-up on the chef's face, overhead of the finished bowl, final side-macro on the first chopstick lift. No shot held longer than 1.5 seconds except the money shot. All handheld. Nothing steady.

LIGHTING: Single strip of warm yellow overhead fluorescent — brutal, honest light. Red lantern glow from the street warms one side of the chef's face. The broth vat glows amber from its own heat. Steam diffuses everything into a haze. No beauty lighting. This is real.
