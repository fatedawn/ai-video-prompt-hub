# REFERENCE ASSIGNMENT

## 提示词（English）

```text
REFERENCE ASSIGNMENT
@Image 1 = Character Lock (Primary & Only Subject)
One single anthropomorphic rabbit athlete. No duplicate character ever appears on screen simultaneously. Identical face, fur pattern, ear shape, whiskers, eye color, body proportions and anatomy must be maintained across every frame and every transformation.
ACE Persona (from @Image 1, left):
White polo with black trim, black shorts, red paddle. Confident, playful, explosive energy.

ZEN Persona (from @Image 1, right):
Navy polo, navy shorts, black paddle, white towel draped or tucked. Calm, precise, disciplined energy.
Only costume, paddle, posture, and expression switch between personas. Face, body, and anatomy never change.

@Image 2 = Environment Lock
Exact Downtown Ping Pong Club: regulation STIGA table, wooden bench seating, industrial dark wall panels, exposed ceiling beams, warm tungsten pendant lights (~3200K), dark wood floor with subtle volumetric haze. Room layout, table position, and lighting rig must not shift between shots.
FORMAT
15-second cinematic sports short. 16:9. 8K photorealistic. High-contrast tungsten lighting, deep cinematic shadows, realistic physics and cloth motion.

SHOT BREAKDOWN

Shot 1 (0:00–0:03) — The Serve
ACE serves the ball with a confident flick. Locked medium shot, 35mm. Ball crosses the table on a natural arc.
SFX: ball toss, paddle contact, table bounce.
Shot 2A (0:03–0:05) — The Setup Whip Pans
Camera stays static in position, whip-panning left and right to follow the ball's crossing rhythm across the table. No speed-streak or lightning-speed effect on the rabbit is visible yet — this section reads as a normal, tense rally building tension before the reveal. Fast directional blur on each pan, but the rabbit's movement itself still looks grounded and real-time.

[Cut]

Shot 2B (0:05–0:08) — The Reveal
Static medium hold, eye-level angle, two-shot, center framing — both ends of the table visible in one locked frame. ACE starts positioned on the left side of frame and strikes the ball. Immediately after the hit, he sprints at lightning speed toward the right side of frame — a clear, readable running motion with speed trails, not a static blur. As he arrives on the right side, he has transformed into ZEN, now in navy outfit with black paddle. ZEN strikes the ball, then sprints back at the same lightning speed toward the left side of frame — again a clear running motion — arriving back on the left side transformed once more into ACE in white-and-black outfit. This hit-then-sprint-then-transform cycle (left to right, then right to left) repeats until the shot transitions into the ending sequence, always keeping the sprint itself readable as running rather than a full smear, so the audience clearly tracks one character alternating sides and personas at incredible speed. Camera remains perfectly static throughout so the speed reads purely through the character's motion, not camera movement.
VFX: speed trails along each sprint, motion blur concentrated during the transformation moment, kinetic air distortion.
[Cut]

Shot 3 (0:08–0:15) — The Smash & Impact
Static medium shot, center framing, low-angle POV from table surface level, with the net visible in the foreground close to camera. ACE emerges from the final sprint, leaps into 15% slow motion, full-body torque, and delivers an explosive overhead smash directly toward camera. The instant the smash connects, motion snaps back to real-time speed as the ball rockets toward the lens and strikes it.
VFX: glass-shatter impact, lens cracking outward.
SFX: heavy paddle impact, rushing air, ball whoosh, glass shatter.
Hard cut to black.

AUDIO
No music. No score. No instruments.
Only diegetic SFX: footsteps, rapid air whooshes, speed-trail hiss, paddle impacts, ball bounces, cloth movement, glass shatter — followed by one loud crowd cheer after the cut to black.

CONTINUITY RULES
Identical face, anatomy, and fur from @Image 1 in every shot — zero identity drift, zero flicker.
Only ONE physical rabbit exists at any moment; never render ACE and ZEN as two separate bodies on screen together.

Shot 2A must NOT show speed-streak/lightning effects — that reveal is reserved exclusively for Shot 2B.
Shot 2B sequence must always follow: hit ball → sprint (readable running motion, not a static blur) → arrive on opposite side already transformed. Never reverse this order (i.e. never transform before or during the sprint's start).

ACE always occupies the left side of frame; ZEN always occupies the right side of frame, consistent across every cycle.

Costume/paddle/prop swap is complete only upon arrival at the opposite side, synced to the end of each sprint.
Shot 3's low-angle table-surface POV must keep the net visible in foreground throughout, until the ball impact breaks the frame.
No subtitles, no on-screen text.
```
